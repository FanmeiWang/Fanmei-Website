Teaching & Training

Teaching Philosophy

My introduction to teaching and learning in higher education occurred during my undergraduate studies in China, where the traditional approach reflects Confucian educational ideology and culture. I sought to demonstrate self-discipline, diligence, respect, courtesy, and thoughtfulness in words and deeds, and I approached schooling as a chance to cultivate these qualities. Professors often teach by establishing theoretical frameworks that enable holistic, systematic thinking—the trees and the forest. The curriculum builds a comprehensive foundation and cultivates logical, rigorous reasoning. These habits of care and thoughtfulness now serve me well as an academic and instructor.

My understanding of possibilities expanded during my M.A. at Laurentian University when I first encountered interactive, collaborative, and exploratory learning and experimented with it as a teaching assistant. During my Ph.D. at Peking University I taught a seminar for American students—Contemporary Issues in China—at IES Abroad’s Beijing Center using literature, workshops, guest lectures, discussion, and field observation. I witnessed how interactive pedagogy can spark students’ profound re-thinking.

Later, at the University of Science and Technology Beijing (USTB), I taught for almost a decade (lecturer → assistant professor) and coordinated the International Graduate Program while the School of Economics and Management pursued AMBA and AACSB accreditation. Supported by international collaborations and reforms, I continued to develop a student-centred pedagogy. From these contexts I distilled four points that guide my practice below.

Linking Theory and Real-World Applications
Creating Proactive and Interactive Learning Environments
Valuing Students as Individuals and Respecting Their Voices
Embracing Teaching as a Continual Process of Learning, Progress, and Growth
